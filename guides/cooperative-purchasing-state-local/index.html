Cooperative Purchasing for State and Local Government

Cooperative purchasing can let eligible public entities buy through established contract vehicles instead of conducting a separate full procurement for every purchase. Vendors should understand where their contracts may be available and which entities are eligible to use them.

Start NAT-CORP onboarding

Why cooperative purchasing matters

Public buyers often look for procurement methods that reduce administrative burden, accelerate purchasing and use competitively established contract terms. For vendors, cooperative access can change how an opportunity is identified and pursued.

What vendors should verify

Contract vehicle

Identify the awarded contract, participating cooperative or authorized schedule and the products or services actually covered.

Eligible buyers

Confirm whether the specific state, local, educational or public entity is eligible to purchase through that program.

Ordering rules

Review the applicable program, contract and buyer requirements rather than assuming every entity purchases the same way.

Scope alignment

Ensure the requested work fits the awarded scope and contract terms before representing a cooperative route as available.

Current GSA state/local programs

GSA states that eligible state and local governments—including instrumentalities such as public schools and water authorities—may access Multiple Award Schedule contracts through specific programs including Cooperative Purchasing, Disaster Purchasing and Public Health Emergency purchasing, subject to program eligibility. Check current GSA eligibility guidance.
