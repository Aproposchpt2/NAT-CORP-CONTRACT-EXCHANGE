State and Local Government Contracts for Businesses

State and local procurement is decentralized. The controlling rules, vendor-registration steps, deadlines and submission methods come from the issuing jurisdiction and solicitation—not from one nationwide portal.

Start NAT-CORP onboarding

What counts as state and local contracting?

Businesses may encounter opportunities from states, counties, cities, public authorities, school districts, universities and other public entities. Procurement structures differ substantially across jurisdictions, so discovery and compliance require source-by-source verification.

How to approach the market

1. Define capability fit

Map products, services, geography, licenses and capacity to the public buyers most likely to purchase them.

2. Maintain registrations

Track each jurisdiction's vendor portal, commodity classifications, contact records and renewal requirements.

3. Read the solicitation

Submission instructions, deadlines, mandatory meetings, certifications and attachments are opportunity-specific.

4. Qualify before bidding

Evaluate scope, schedule, mandatory requirements, pricing risk and delivery capacity before committing pursuit resources.

Federal and SLED are different search territories

NAT-CORP focuses on state/local/SLED opportunity matching. Federal contract matching belongs to the Registered Federal Contractors Portal.

Reference

GSA maintains specific programs through which eligible state and local entities may access certain federal supply schedules, including Cooperative Purchasing, Disaster Purchasing and Public Health Emergency programs. Eligibility depends on the applicable program and entity. Review current GSA state/local guidance.
